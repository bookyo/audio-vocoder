What it does

Two signals. One voice.

A vocoder is not a pitch shifter, a filter, or a voice changer. It is a cross-synthesis effect: it reads the spectral envelope of one signal and applies it to the timbre of another. Your speech supplies the articulation; the carrier supplies the sound.

The modulator

Typically a voice or instrument recording. The vocoder tracks its energy across frequency bands — the syllable shapes, consonants, and pitch movement that make speech intelligible.

The carrier

A synthesized signal that provides the timbre. Synth, noise, pulse, and harmonic-rich carriers each give a different character, from clean electronic to gritty alien.

Not Auto-Tune. Not a voice changer.

Auto-Tune corrects the pitch of one voice while keeping its timbre. A voice changer shifts or filters one stream. A vocoder always combines two streams: the syllables of the modulator and the tone of the carrier. That is why the output sounds like an instrument talking.

The controls

Every parameter, explained.

The live tool exposes eight controls. These are the defaults and the practical direction for each one.

Modulator Audio File

required

The voice or instrument to be vocoded. Common audio formats are accepted; the upload limit is 100 MB.

Tip: close-mic, dry, crisp consonants produce the clearest results.

Carrier Type

synth

Synth is clean and electronic, noise is gritty, pulse is sharp and robotic, and harmonic-rich is fuller and organ-like.

Noise and percussion carriers are rhythmic but whispery; simple tonal carriers carry speech better.

Carrier Base Frequency

100 Hz

Range 20–1000 Hz. Lower values give a deeper voice; higher values sound brighter and thinner.

Try 80–150 Hz for a natural robot range; push higher for a character voice.

Carrier Waveform

sawtooth

Sine is pure, sawtooth is bright, square is harsh, and triangle is soft. The waveform shapes the harmonic character of the carrier.

Sawtooth and square are the classic choices for intelligible robot vocals.

Frequency Bands

16

Range 4–32. More bands give clearer articulation; fewer bands give a smoother, more synthetic texture.

If words are getting lost, raise the band count before changing anything else.

Analysis Window

50 ms

Range 10–200 ms. Shorter windows are more responsive; longer windows smooth transitions.

The tool derives its envelope-follower frequency from this value: 1000 ÷ window in ms.

Wet/Dry Mix

0.8

Range 0–1. At 0 you hear the original; at 1 you hear only the vocoded signal.

Use 0.8–1.0 for a full effect; blend lower if you want the original voice to remain present.

Output Format

MP3

MP3, AAC, M4A, OGG, Opus, FLAC, or WAV. Choose lossless FLAC/WAV for further editing; MP3 for quick sharing.

The result is a rendered file, not a live audio stream.

How it works

From a dry take to a talking machine.

1 · Prepare the modulator

Record or choose a dry voice clip. Keep it close to the mic, remove long silences, and keep the consonants crisp. The vocoder can only work with the articulation you give it.

2 · Choose the carrier

Pick a carrier type, frequency, and waveform. A sustained sawtooth pad is the classic; noise and pulse give a harder, more mechanical edge.

3 · Tune and render

Set the band count, analysis window, wet/dry mix, and output format. Run the tool, audition the result, and adjust one parameter at a time.

Under the hood, the implementation is a channel vocoder: the modulator and carrier are split into logarithmically spaced bands from 100 Hz to 5 kHz, each modulator band’s envelope is extracted, the matching carrier band is multiplied by that envelope, and the bands are summed and mixed with the dry signal.

Fit and limits

What it is — and what it is not.

Best for

  • Robot-voice hooks and vocoded vocals for electronic music.
  • Stylizing a voice for sci-fi shorts, games, or anonymous narration.
  • Comparing carriers, waveforms, and band counts before a final render.

Not for

  • Cleaning up a noisy recording — a vocoder adds character; it does not remove noise.
  • Live microphone processing — this tool renders an uploaded file.
  • Uploading a custom carrier — the carrier is synthesized only.

Watch out

  • Files over 100 MB: trim or split the recording first.
  • Muffled output: raise the band count toward 32 and try a sawtooth or square carrier.
  • Weak consonants: re-record close to the mic and enunciate clearly.

FAQ

Questions, answered.

What is a vocoder effect?

A vocoder effect analyzes the spectral envelope of a modulator signal and applies it to a carrier signal, creating robotic or electronic voice sounds.

What audio formats are supported?

Input supports common audio formats such as MP3 and WAV. Output can be MP3, AAC, M4A, OGG, Opus, FLAC, or WAV.

Can I use my own carrier signal?

No. The tool uses synthesized carriers — synth, noise, pulse, or harmonic-rich. You cannot upload a custom carrier file; use your recording as the modulator instead.

How do I make the vocoded voice clearer?

Increase the number of frequency bands for clearer articulation, decrease it for a smoother effect, and adjust the analysis window for responsiveness. Crisp consonants in the source matter more than any single setting.

Is there a file size limit?

Yes. The modulator audio file must be under 100 MB. Trim or split longer recordings first.

How do I vocode my voice onto a song or instrumental?

Split the material in two: the song or chord progression becomes the carrier, and your voice becomes the modulator. Load both and render. For the classic effect, use a sustained synth pad as the carrier and speak with exaggerated consonants — consonants are what keep vocoded words intelligible.

Why does my output sound muffled?

Intelligibility lives mostly in consonants such as s, t, and k. If the modulator is mumbled, far from the microphone, or drenched in reverb, the vocoder has little to work with. Re-record close to the mic, raise the band count, and avoid busy carriers: a simple sustained tone carries speech better than a full mix.

What makes a good carrier?

Steady, harmonically rich, tonal sources work best: sawtooth or square synth pads, organ chords, and string ensembles excite all frequency bands evenly. Noise and percussion carriers give rhythmic but whispery results; full songs sound muddy because the vocoder bands fight with the music’s own chords.

Can I control the effect live with gestures?

This tool renders uploaded audio rather than processing live input, so gesture control happens by recording it: speak or hum while moving, and use a dynamic source so the sweeps get captured. For true real-time hand control you need a hardware vocoder pedal or a live plugin.

Is a vocoder the same as Auto-Tune?

No. Auto-Tune corrects the pitch of a voice but keeps its timbre. A voice changer shifts or filters a single audio stream. A vocoder combines two streams: it reads the syllables of one and speaks them through the timbre of another.
